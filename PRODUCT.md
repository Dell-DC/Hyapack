## Users

Primary: B2B brand buyers and procurement/packaging decision-makers at companies (e.g. cosmetics, skincare, lotion, personal-care brands) evaluating HYAPACK as a modular protective-packaging supplier. They arrive needing to be convinced of technical capability (a modular block system configured to their product's dimensions, without new mould tooling per SKU), sustainability credibility, and a clear path to start a custom project.

Secondary (not the primary optimization target for this pass): customers who have already received HYAPACK packaging and want to know how to reuse/repurpose/dispose of it responsibly — served by the separate Multi-Use Guide page.

## Product Purpose

HYAPACK transforms harvested, invasive water hyacinth into a modular protective packaging system that replaces selected single-use plastic packaging (foam, plastic inserts, bubble wrap) for products such as lotion bottles and jars. Rather than casting one bespoke mould per product, HYAPACK builds each holder from a kit of standardised fibre blocks (columns, panels, bases, connectors) arranged around the product's shape. Success = a prospective brand buyer understands the material story, sees that a protective, custom-fit holder can be assembled from modular blocks without new mould tooling for every product, trusts the sustainability and sourcing claims (stated conservatively), and takes a next step (explore the Multi-Use Guide or view the packaging / start a custom inquiry).

## Positioning

A modular protective-packaging system — standardised water-hyacinth-fibre blocks that are arranged and combined into a custom-fit holder per product, rather than a new mould cast for every SKU — made from fibre harvested during waterway cleanup. This combination (modular, reconfigurable fit + a named sourcing community and cleanup activity in Ayutthaya/Bang Sai) is a story a generic "eco packaging" or single-mould moulded-pulp competitor could not truthfully copy.

Core line: "Transforming invasive water hyacinth into modular, protective and multi-use packaging."

## Operating Context

- Product lifecycle framing used throughout the site: **Protect → Reuse → Repurpose → Compost**.
- Technical/configuration workflow: customer provides product dimensions/CAD → HYAPACK selects and arranges standard fibre blocks (columns, panels, bases, connectors) into a holder layout that cushions and locates the product → configuration is fixed/verified → manufacture. This replaces the earlier single-mould-per-product approach; no new mould tooling is required to fit a new product, only a new block arrangement.
- Sourcing workflow: **Harvest → Sort → Clean → Dry → Process → Mould → Deliver**, sourced from Ayutthaya (Bang Sai area), partnering with local community enterprise "วิสาหกิจชุมชนบ้านผักตบชวาไม้ตรา", waterway-cleanup groups, municipalities, OTOP enterprises.
- Site is two pages for this pass: a landing/overview page (`index.html`/home route) and a separate, more detailed **Multi-Use Guide** page for reuse/repurpose/dispose instructions. Homepage links out to the guide rather than duplicating its detail.
- English-only content for now; other languages (e.g. Thai) may follow later — do not build a language switcher unless asked.

## Product Principles

1. **Credibility over promotion.** Every environmental or social claim uses the softened, testable phrasing above; nothing is stated as certified, guaranteed, or 100% unless it actually is.
2. **Modular fit is the technical proof point.** The block-configuration story (customer dimensions → arranged modular blocks → no new mould tooling per product) is what differentiates HYAPACK from generic eco-packaging and single-mould moulded-pulp competitors, and should be given real technical weight, not just a marketing mention.
3. **Lifecycle over single-use.** Protect → Reuse → Repurpose → Compost is the narrative spine connecting the hero, product section, and the separate Multi-Use Guide.
4. **Traceable sourcing, named place.** Ayutthaya/Bang Sai and the community-enterprise partnership are concrete, named details that ground the sustainability story — use them rather than generic "eco-friendly" language.
5. **Real assets only.** Use the supplied images where they fit; everywhere else, use a clearly marked placeholder rather than inventing photography, names, or data.
